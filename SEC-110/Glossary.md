**WEEK 1**
- *The internet* is just the physical network of all the devices and computers connected to each other
-  *The web* is the collection of information that is accessed through the internet
- *Cyberspace* is (the combination of everything tech) a global domain within the information environment consisting of interdependent networks of information systems including the internet, telecommunications networks, computer systems, embedded processors and controllers.
- *Cybersecurity* is defined as the prevention of damage to, protection of, and restoration of computers, networks, electronic communication, etc. Also known as the ability to protect or defend from cyber attacks
  
**WEEK 2**
- *The CIA Triad* is the three primary aspects of protected information in cyber security (confidentiality, integrity, availability)
- *Confidentiality* refers to the property that information is only available/accessible to those who are authorized
- *Integrity* is the assurance that information is accurate, trustworthy, and only changed by the proper sources
- *Availability* is the idea that information remains accessible to the authorized parties
- *Software vulnerabilities* are flaws, bugs, glitches, or weaknesses found in software that can be exploited
- Cyber threats are an attempt to damage or disrupt a computer network or system, either physically or software, human or not.
- *A risk matrix* is a visual that categorizes threats by both how likely they are and how bad it would be if they were to happen 
- *Supply chain attacks* are attacks that are meant to slow down, stop, or increase costs of production.
- *Spear phishing* is a type of phishing attack targeted at specific individuals or organizations rather than general phishing attempts

**Week 3**
- *OSINT* stands for open source intelligence, and refers to searching for information on public, open-source places that could be used for causing a potential harm (to a person or an organization)
-  *Data Abstractions* are the different levels in which data is stored
-  *HIPAA* refers to the laws surrounding the protections of people's healthcare information
-  *CFAA* refers to the laws around misusing government information
-  *Controls* are the different ways in which organizations can ensure the right people have access to the right things

**Week 4**
- The *OSI Model* is a breakdown of how data is transferred between computers and networks
- The *Physical Layer* is the first OSI layer, and it deals with the actual physical transmission of data — cables, wires, radio waves, and the hardware components of devices. Doesn't care about what the data is, just moves signals.
- The *Data Link Layer* is the second OSI layer, and it handles how data gets sent between devices on the same network. Uses MAC addresses to identify devices.
- The *Network Layer* is the third, and possibly most important layer, and it handles routing data between different networks using IP addresses.
- The *Transport Layer* is the fourth layer, and it manages end-to-end communication between hosts.
- The *Session Layer* is the fifth layer, and it controls the opening, closing, and security of communication channels between two devices
- The *Presentation Layer* is the sixth layer, and it handles data formatting, security, and compression.
- The *Application Layer* is the seventh layer, and it allows the software user and the network to interact directly.
- The *TCP/IP model* is a network framework with multiple layers that explains how data is communicated between devices over a network using standardized protocols to ensure reliable and efficient transmission.
